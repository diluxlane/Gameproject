# East Bank · art direction

**A working coastal utility yard after evacuation.** Art-test question: can the player tell what the place did, where its people went and what still works before reading the journal?

## Shot plan

| View | Composition and exposure | Pass criterion |
|---|---|---|
| Title | Frame the receiving dish above roof height. Keep the left third quieter and use the broadcast lettering as a foreground plane. | Dish rim, feed and bearing are separately readable beneath a bright sky; all four menu verbs fit on phone landscape. |
| Shelter | Eye height 1.66 m. Foreground radio, adjacent storage, warm practical, maintenance bench, hard exit into daylight. | Workbench has legs, surface wear and plausible reach. The outside landmark survives exposure changes. |
| Field stores | Toolbox and first aid kit beside service pipes. Plant growth concentrated at edges. | Contents distinguish a stores bench from the pump, even with guide text off. |
| Pump yard | A portable generator below visible pipework and power cabinet. Use asphalt, weathered mortar, painted steel and sunlit edges at different scales. | Water access visibly makes sense here. Check the same yard in reverse after restoring power. |
| Archive | Final instrument at the edge of the mast field. The silhouette beyond the roofline changes only after tuning. | Aiming at the cabinet does not force the player to look away from every landmark. |

## Surface and lighting rules

Use photographic albedo without baked dramatic light wherever possible. Interpret color textures as sRGB; normal, roughness and packed material data stay linear. Use consistent map scale across individual blocks. High-frequency finish belongs on the hand-sized object. Mid-frequency stains, panel seams, bolts and cable routes should survive at ten metres. Beyond the fence, large shape and value carry detail.

Concrete should show a history of water. Metal under cover retains more paint than a dish panel exposed to the sky. Place growth where a joint holds soil. Preserve a navigable service aisle. Future decals should follow drainage, impact or human use; distant moss has no reason to repeat at regular intervals. For roofs and windows, test silhouettes against the sky with all interface elements hidden.

Use one coherent evening sky, one principal shadow direction and a local warm practical. Use image-based lighting for broad reflected color. Preserve shadow detail. Avoid perpetual full-frame bloom, lens dirt and fog overlays. A portable lamp should illuminate its surroundings without bleaching a radio face. Weather progression needs a dry baseline, a rain state and a drying state with consistent roughness, sky and sound.

## Graphic and sonic identity

Cream stock, dark green instrument cases and amber channel markings. Large condensed-looking identity type is set tightly; equipment captions use a monospaced face, chapter titles a quieter serif. Text, focus and disabled states are separate signals. Handheld maps and equipment diagrams should remain legible with reduced motion. Final controller glyphs and subtitles need a physical TV-distance review.

The recurring sound is three low knocks separated by silence. Machinery carries the bass register; dialogue and important pickups leave it. In a later sound pass, record footsteps on wet asphalt, sheet-metal roofing and shelter concrete. A ferry bell outside the stereo image should lead an investigation even before its source is seen.
